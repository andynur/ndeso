## Findings that change the docs now

These are corrections backed by sources, applied in the same PR to
[PLACES](../PLACES.md). None changes a GDD number.

1. **"Balle Al Jannah" is a working business, not just a place name.** It is spelled
   *Balle* by its owners, and it is a venue in Krajan, Baledono that rents space for
   walimahan and meetings and also runs a mini zoo, kiosk rentals and aqiqah services.
   **Masjid Ar Royyan**, which presents itself as Purworejo's Islamic Center, stands
   inside it (Jl. Jend. Gatot Subroto 370). [1][2][3] This makes the naming question
   (Q5) weightier: using the name would borrow a live commercial brand that has a
   mosque in it.
2. **Pasar Baledono is open all day, not just in the morning.** It is listed as open
   04:30–17:00 and holds an SNI Pasar Rakyat certificate. [4][5] It is a rebuilt
   market: the new building has a musala and offices upstairs, and it kept the stall
   sizes it had before a fire. [6] So "shuts by late morning" is wrong for the building.
   What is still true is that fresh produce and the bakul trade peak at dawn. PLACES now
   says so.
3. **The Klenteng Thong Hwie Kiong ("Klenteng Baledono") was missing.** It stands just
   east of Pasar Baledono, was founded in 1888 by Chinese traders, serves Tridharma
   worship (Buddhist, Confucian, Taoist) and is a listed cagar budaya. [7] It is the real
   anchor of the Chinese-Indonesian presence that CULTURE_GUIDE §1.4 asks us to show,
   so we should use it as a landmark rather than inventing one. It is a place of worship,
   so it is visible and non-interactive (CULTURE_GUIDE).
4. **Geger Menjangan is in Trirejo, Kecamatan Loano, not in Baledono.** It is about 5 km
   from the town centre and **175 m above sea level**, and it has an observation tower,
   a playground and a pool. The view takes in the town, the Bogowonto, the Menoreh hills
   and, on a clear day, the south coast. **The grave of Kyai Imam Puro** is on it, and
   people make pilgrimages there, especially before Ramadan. [8][9] The makam must be
   treated as sacred: no foraging near it and no interaction beyond a quiet description.
5. **Baledono lies on the Kali Bogowonto.** The river is its eastern boundary, and the
   whole kelurahan is inside the town area. [10] So a big open field in Baledono is
   fiction made by compressing the map (PLACES §2), which we allow, and the river is a
   real feature right next door. The province's rice belt is further south, in Ngombol,
   Purwodadi and Banyuurip. [11]

## Recommendations for the maintainer (not applied — decide at MVP review)

| # | Recommendation | Why |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Ship the MVP under a name of our own** and keep the `bale` id. Show plain **"Balé"**, or name it after Mbah Hita. Keep "Al Jannah" out of player-facing text until the owners say yes. | Finding 1: it is a live brand with a mosque inside, and the PRD §12.2 permission is still open. A neutral name costs nothing now and avoids renaming after release. No locale string uses "Al Jannah" today. |
| R2 | Call the flagship festival **Merti Desa** in Baledono (in EN: *merti desa, the village thanksgiving*). Keep *sedekah bumi* as a synonym. | Baledono itself calls it *merti desa* and holds it with a **wayang kulit** night. [12][13] |
| R3 | **Question the festival's timing.** The GDD puts it in Kasadasa, after the harvest. Baledono's 2025 merti desa was on **17 January 2025**, which falls in mangsa Kapitu, the wet season. [13] | This is one data point, so change nothing yet. Ask the readers: is the date fixed by a Javanese month (Sura, Ruwah) or by the harvest? |
| R4 | Add **manggis** (mangosteen) next to durian as a Kaligesing tree crop, and consider *vanili*. | Durian, manggis, Kaligesing goats, coffee, *madu klanceng* and vanilla are the regency's flagship products, mostly from Kaligesing. [11][14] |
| R5 | Dolalak (M4): show a **mixed or men's troupe**, not only young women. Keep the Dutch-soldier-uniform look, and treat the **trance (*ndadi*)** as the troupe's own matter, never as spectacle or a jump-scare. | It began with men in black uniforms copied from Dutch soldiers, formalised in Kaliharjo, Kaligesing in 1936. Women have danced it only since about 1970, and the short-trouser costume is a live debate locally ("pupu ler"). [15][16][17][18] |
| R6 | Pranata mangsa: have **older characters use it and younger ones check the BMKG** or the government's planting calendar (Katam). | Sources say it is still used but increasingly adjusted against the BMKG and Katam because of climate shift. The Purworejo agriculture office published an explainer on it. [19][20][21] This matches the GDD's "living, not a museum piece" intent. |
