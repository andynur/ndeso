# CULTURE GUIDE — Representing Indonesia with respect

> Read before writing any NPC, dialog, festival, clothing, food, building, or religious reference.
> Principle: **celebrate, don't caricature.** Culture lives in the *systems* and the *people*, not in exotic decoration.

## 1. Principles

1. **A real place, named carefully.** Balé is set in **Baledono, Purworejo, Jawa Tengah** — not a fictional composite. That is what makes it feel true, and it is also what makes carelessness costly. The naming policy (which real names we may use, which we must fictionalise, and what needs permission) lives in **[PLACES §1](PLACES.md)** and is binding.
2. **People are people first.** An NPC's regional or religious identity is a background detail, not a punchline or their whole personality.
3. **No sacred things as loot.** Sacred objects, prayers, and rituals are never items to sell, farm, or grind.
4. **Portray the diversity that is actually there.** Baledono is a Javanese, overwhelmingly Muslim kelurahan — writing it otherwise would be a different kind of falsehood. Its real diversity is local: musholla on the lanes and a masjid agung in town, a church in Purworejo (a colonial-era town), a Chinese-Indonesian trading presence around the pasar that is part of the fabric rather than a token. Show what is there; do not import a diversity checklist, and do not flatten the place into one note either.
5. **Ask the community.** Anything regional gets reviewed by at least one person from that region before v1. Because the setting is a real kelurahan, **this review happens before M2 writing begins**, not at M4. Use the *Culture feedback* issue template.

## 3. Religion & spirituality

The player character is **Muslim and Javanese**, and so is nearly everyone
around them, because that is Baledono. The flavour is specifically **Javanese
Islam of the Kedu/Mataraman kind** — traditionalist, warm, comfortable with
slametan and tahlilan — not a generic or imported Islam.

### 3.1 The line: characterisation, never scoring

**Worship is never a mechanic.** No faith meter, no `+5 pahala`, no
button-prompt prayer, no reward or penalty for observance. The game never
checks whether the player prays.

What it *does* do is let religious practice shape the world's rhythm, because
that is simply what the place is like:

- **Prayer times are the clock.** The HUD reads `15:40 · Ashar` because that is
  how people there tell the time (GDD §3.2). It is a **display**: it gates
  nothing, scores nothing, requires nothing.
- **Adzan is ambience.** Audio defaults off for players outside Indonesia
  (locale ≠ `id`), at low volume. The *labels* stay for everyone, with glossary
  entries. It is never a gameplay timer or a trigger.
- **Ramadan changes the town, not the player** (GDD §10). Market hours shift,
  midday empties, takjil stalls appear, tarawih fills the musholla. The game
  does not model the player's fasting at all.
- **Lebaran** is mudik, sungkeman, ketupat, and the kampung filling up again.
  It is the emotional peak of the year and needs no mechanic beyond that.

### 3.2 "Al Jannah" — how to frame it

The starting location is named for the garden of paradise, and the game's goal
is to make the ground *asri, nyaman, tenang*.

⚠️ **Never frame this as building paradise.** Jannah is God's to give, not a
human project, and writing it otherwise is both presumptuous and inaccurate to
how people actually speak. The correct framing — and the true one — is that an
old man hoped to make a place that **reminds** people of that garden. Aspiration
and homage, in one person's voice. Keep every line of dialog on that side of it.

### 3.3 Contested practices

Slametan, tahlilan, yasinan, ziarah and *weton* are ordinary in a Javanese
kampung, and some Muslims consider them *bid'ah*. Both views are held sincerely
by real people.

- Portray them as **what this village does** — warmly, matter-of-factly.
- **Never stage a fiqh debate.** No character argues the point; the game takes
  no side and issues no verdict.
- **Weton is a date, not a divination.** Use it as a personal day like a
  birthday. No *petung*, no fortune-telling, no luck mechanics.
- Expect objections anyway, and answer them with this section rather than by
  quietly changing the content.

### 3.4 Other guidance

- **Dewi Sri:** revered in Javanese agrarian tradition, and still meaningful to many people. Represent her as a gentle, dignified presence in lore (a story Mbah Sri tells, a phrase said at planting). **Do not** make her a quest-giver who trades items for power ups, a romance option, or a comic figure. Any "harvest spirit" gameplay role goes to something **clearly our own invention**, named as such.
- **Places of worship:** visible, respectful, non-interactive (or with a simple "the building is quiet" description). No items or events inside. The musholla and the masjid agung are **social landmarks** — people gather outside them — which is different from making the prayer hall itself a game space.
- **Food & animals:** no pork items and no alcohol in MVP content. This is not squeamishness; it is what a warung in Baledono actually sells.
- **Mystical content:** light folklore (a *kuntilanak* rumour, *tuyul* jokes) only as NPC stories and never mocking believers. No black magic mechanics.

## 4. NPCs in a Javanese kampung

- The cast is predominantly Javanese because the place is. Do not scatter
  regional identities across it for variety's sake — an invented Balinese or
  Bugis resident in Baledono is a *worse* representation, not a better one.
  Newcomers and returnees are the honest way to widen the cast.
- Avoid stereotypes: Batak = loud, Padang = stingy, Chinese-Indonesian = only a shopkeeper, Madurese = hot-tempered, Papuan = "primitive". **Never.**
- Koh Hendra (Chinese-Indonesian) runs a shop *and* is a local-history buff and a community figure. The Chinese-Indonesian trading presence around a Javanese pasar is real and long-standing; write him as a neighbour, not a category.
- **Elders are not a single note.** Mbah Hita is the anchor of the whole game: he has appetites, jokes, stubbornness and regrets, not just wisdom.
- Keep the cast varied in gender and age. Young women are not only love interests.
- Names and honorifics: *Pak/Bu* general; *Mas/Mbak* for Javanese peers; *Mbah* for elders; *Kang* familiar; *Koh/Cik* Chinese-Indonesian. Purworejo names are ordinary Javanese names — resist the exotic.

## 5. Language in dialog

- The system UI is standard Indonesian (see DESIGN §9).
- **Speech level is characterisation.** Javanese has *ngoko* and *krama*, and
  who uses which to whom says more than a paragraph of description: the player
  speaks **krama** to Mbah Hita and he answers in **ngoko**; peers use ngoko
  both ways; the kelurahan office gets krama. Get this right and Javanese
  players feel the relationships instantly. Get it wrong and it reads as rude.
- The local dialect is **Bagelen** — transitional between Banyumasan and
  Mataraman, neither full ngapak nor Jogja-Solo. Listeners from the area notice.
- NPC dialog may use Javanese words (*monggo*, *nuwun sewu*, *matur nuwun*, *nggih*) and must stay understandable to a non-Javanese Indonesian reader. Every such word goes in the glossary in the same PR.
- **EN translation:** keep culturally loaded nouns in Indonesian or Javanese (italic), and translate everything else naturally. Don't over-exoticise. The *Kamus* carries the load — which is why it moves up to M2.
- No profanity beyond very mild expressions (*astaga*, *aduh*, *waduh*, *lha*).

## 6. History & politics

- Independence Day (*Tujuhbelasan*) is joyful and communal. Keep it apolitical: no parties, politicians, or contemporary controversies.
- Avoid real contemporary political figures, parties, regional conflicts, and ethnic tensions entirely.
